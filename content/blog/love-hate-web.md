I have just returned from a month-long, laptop-free vacation. When I returned to the keyboard, it took me two hours and a stiff drink to recover from the barrage of small frustrations.

How could we let modern tech get so bad? I hate that I can't find anything with Google anymore. I hate that AI has filled every space with noise. I hate constantly proving to CloudFlare that I am human. I hate getting the same personalized recommendations as everyone. I hate being interrupted by layers of two-factor auth. I hate getting nothing but LLM-generated outreach emails.

The web has become a [market for lemons](https://www.fortressofdoors.com/ai-markets-for-lemons-and-the-great-logging-off/), and I so badly wanted to log off forever.

But there are still parts of the internet that fill me to the gills with joy. I love [blogrolls](https://indieweb.org/blogroll) and human curation. I love email correspondence and especially reader mail. I love websites made by passionate people like [Emma Goto](https://www.emgoto.com/) and [Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/). I love the blogs I find on [Bear](https://bearblog.dev/discover/) and [Bubbles](https://bubbles.town/). I love weird, whimsy, poetic websites.

I want to find those who create in the quieter parts of the internet and live among them. I want to spawn small communities of kind and helpful people. I want to be a loudly curious [artist-in-residence of my world](https://amystewart.substack.com/p/be-the-artist-in-resident-of-planet).
