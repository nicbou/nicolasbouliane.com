Immigrants need a space to discuss the challenges of settling in Germany. Those discussions should happen in public, so that others may benefit. That space should feel welcoming and helpful.

I am building a community around [All About Berlin](/projects/all-about-berlin). It will be a forum where immigrants get help from experts and peers.

## Openness

Currently, most immigration talk happens in private Facebook groups, Telegram channels and WhatsApp group chats. The knowledge is shared once then lost forever. It's invisible to outsiders, and often even to insiders, lost in a stream of ads and suggested content.

Those who would benefit the most from that knowledge will never find it.

Reddit is a notable exception - it's a public forum - but that might not last. They are providing the AI training data for the models that aim to replace them. Bots are infesting its communities. Sooner or later, they will also go private.[^1]

All discussions on this forum will be public. My explicit goal is to amplify the wisdom of the crowd. Helpful answers will be accessible to all, without restrictions, including through AI agents.

## Kindness

The moderation for this new community should foster a culture of kindness and helpfulness. Snarky or unhelpful comments should be quietly removed. Repeat offenders should be shown the door. Behaviour that would not be tolerated at a meetup should not be tolerated in an online community.

I put a lot of faith in good moderation. Moderators should act more like librarians than like bouncers. They should patiently guide people to existing answers and make newcomers feel at home.

## Helpfulness

In due time, experts should have special profiles. Contributions from lawyers, relocation consultants and the like should be highlighted.

I see a way to align incentives. A lawyer can gain visibility and find new clients by being a helpful contributor.

## And how will we pay for that?

Most likely, we won't. This community will not be profitable, but it also won't cost a lot to run. I expect it to stay a small and cosy corner of the web.

We could probably generate a small income by wrapping product mentions with affiliate links. A Commerzbank mention could turn into an affiliate link to their website, so if someone opens a bank account, it would go towards our hosting and moderation bills.

## Fears

A few fears have kept me from starting this community earlier.

My biggest fear is dealing with all the drama. Moderation is unpleasant, thankless work. I don't want the stress of interpersonal conflict to seep into my personal life.

My second biggest fear is the time commitment. All About Berlin never requires my immediate attention. I work on what feels right, when it feels right. A community requires constant monitoring. I don't want to get dragged out of a vacation by internet drama.

I was planning to have paid moderation, but my discretionary budget has [dwindled significantly](/blog/death-by-ai), so I must handle moderation myself.

## Why now?

I should have started a community years ago. When ToyTown Germany vanished, I should have filled the gap. We would have a wonderful, vibrant community by now.

The second best time is today.

The conditions are far from perfect. We're facing stronger headwinds. How will we handle the relentless bot traffic? Will anyone join the community if they get all their answers from Claude? Will Google surface our answers at all?

Frankly, I just don't know. I am scared, but this is important to me, so I will do it anyway.

[^1]: The [dark forest theory](https://maggieappleton.com/cozy-web) of the web: a web full of predatory bots forces communities to go into hiding.
